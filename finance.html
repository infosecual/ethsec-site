Team and compensation

A small senior core, a planned bench.

In the base model today

The founder and a senior researcher, both from month one, on senior contracts at the level this talent commands on the top contest platforms. The model also funds one additional researcher from bounty income by month 24.

Why so senior

Throughput scales with compute, not headcount. A few elite researchers at the end of the funnel verify and file what the models surface; the pipeline does the bulk read-and-triage. The spend per high or critical finding, not the salary line, is the number to watch.

Proposed · layered on the base model

Expanded hiring plan

On top of the two-senior core, the plan adds a small analyst bench to handle bug-filing operations and reporting throughput, at much lower cost per head, and a third senior researcher once bounty income and donations are flowing, so that hire is funded by results rather than by the raise.

Funding path: the base case already carries about a $3.4M buffer and a mechanism that applies half of bounty income to extra hires. A phased expansion fits inside that, with the analysts and the third researcher coming on as donated credits and bounty income land, and the third researcher specifically when a second anchor commitment or a steady bounty run-rate supports it. Pulling hires forward trims the roughly 12 months of post-raise runway accordingly. These are planning estimates.

Structure

A for-profit path, optional.

The structure can support a for-profit subsidiary wholly owned by the non-profit, with the non-profit's intellectual property licensed to it for commercial use. Any surplus returns to the non-profit and its mission. This is an option under review, subject to legal and governance sign-off, not a commitment.

Burn, runway, and the compute thesis

The shape of the spend.

  • Peak monthly net burn is about $0.62M, in month two, driven by the one-time hardware buy. Year-two average net burn settles near $275K a month.
  • Compute (tokens, hardware, benchmarking) is the largest and most flexible line. Frontier tokens run about $3.5M in year one and $4.5M in year two (base), scaling with review volume; in-kind credits cover roughly 45% of gross token spend.
  • Hardware is a small one-time buy, about $225K for a GPU server and a 512GB workstation, validated on rented cloud GPUs first. Owned rigs carry the high-volume reading at near-zero marginal cost.
  • Donated credits are the biggest swing factor. Their size and timing move the net cash required more than any other input, which is why securing them early is a first-order goal.
  • Bounties trend the effort toward self-funding. Winnings recycle into compute and headcount and never choose what to hunt; direction is set by criticality.

Assumptions and notes

Reading the numbers.

  • Figures are the base scenario of a 24-month cash-flow model. Low and high scenarios vary the token run-rate, credit size and timing, and bounty income.
  • Researchers are modeled as contractors. Employer payroll tax, benefits, and platform fees are either off or folded into overhead.
  • Bounty income is treated as compute credits against token spend rather than cash to the organization, so the budget does not lean on it.
  • Capital is assumed received up front from an anchor donor. The model also supports phased receipts.
